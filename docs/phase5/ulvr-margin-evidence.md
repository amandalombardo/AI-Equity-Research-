# Unilever margin evidence (for brief assumption C), 13 October 2026

**Question:** what operating margin should the DCF use for the Unilever that remains after Foods is sold?

## 1. Starting point: FY25 without the sale perimeter
| €m | Group (continuing, ex Ice Cream) | Sale perimeter | Remaining Unilever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turnover | 50,503 | 10,700 | **39,803** |
| Underlying operating profit | 10,084 | 2,600 | **7,484** |
| Underlying operating margin | 20.0% | 24% | **18.8%** |

Sources: Group from the FY25 results 6-K (0001654954-26-001155, note 2); perimeter "€10.7bn sales, UOP €2.6bn, UOM 24%" from the Foods/McCormick investor presentation (Form 425, 0000217410-26-000027; "preliminary carve-out financial information"). Management's own pro forma for the remaining company is "Turnover €39bn ... Underlying Operating Margin >19%" (same presentation). Our 18.8% is slightly below that, because the perimeter figures are rounded to €0.1bn.

**Stranded costs (corrected 13 Oct 2026, see mistakes log row 19):** an earlier version of this pack said no figure was disclosed. That was wrong. The deal presentation (Form 425, 0000217410-26-000027, "Clear plan to manage costs") and the 31 March call (Form 425, 0000217410-26-000032) say: **"c.€400-500m gross stranded costs"**, mitigated by **"c.€500m one-off restructuring ... to be incurred over 2027-2029"**, with "no operational dis-synergies" and transitional service agreements (TSAs) covering the gap. The CFO: "we are not calling any margin dilution arising from these stranded costs." So management expects the 18.8% to hold once the €500m is spent.

## 2. Track record of the underlying margin (Group, as reported each year)
| FY17 | FY18 | FY19 | FY20 | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 (ex Ice Cream) | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17.5% | 18.4% | 19.1% | 18.5% | 18.4% | 16.1% | 16.7% | 18.4% | 20.0% | 20.3% |

Sources: annual results 6-Ks FY17-FY25, H1 2026 6-K. FY22-23 fell with input-cost inflation, then recovered. Management says the remaining company's margin rose about 170bps from 2023 to 2025 (Form 425 investor presentation, "Underlying operating margin expansion 2023 to 2025 ~ +170bps"), so on that basis it was roughly 17.3% in 2023 (our inference: 19.0% less 1.7 points). FY26 guidance: "modest improvement" on 20.0% (Group).

## 3. Restructuring is a recurring cost, not a one-off
"Underlying" profit excludes restructuring, but Unilever has paid it every year:

| | FY16 | FY17 | FY18 | FY19 | FY20 | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Restructuring, €m | 578 | 638 | 914 | 1,159 | 916 | 632 | 777 | 425 | 710 | 599 |
| % of turnover | 1.1% | 1.2% | 1.8% | 2.2% | 1.8% | 1.2% | 1.3% | 0.8% | 1.4% | 1.2% |

Source: SEC XBRL ifrs-full:ExpenseOfRestructuringActivities and ifrs-full:Revenue, latest 20-F reporting each year (FY23-25 restated without Ice Cream, FY25 20-F 0000217410-26-000007). Ten-year average 1.4%; last three years 1.1%. Management said restructuring would return "to pre-2017 levels of around 1% of turnover" (FY21 results release, 0001654954-22-001398).

**Management's new guide:** "Excluding this one-off, we are maintaining restructuring at the rate of about 0.6% of turnover per annum" (CFO, 31 March 2026 call, Form 425 0000217410-26-000032). In none of the last ten years was it that low: the lowest was 0.8% (FY23), and after the 2021 guide of "around 1%" the actual was 0.8-1.4%.

So a DCF that uses the underlying margin and ignores restructuring overstates cash flow by about 1 point of sales a year. This is the same issue as Diageo's €850m restructuring cash, except that at Unilever it never stops.

## 4. Decision (agreed 13 Oct 2026, re-checked after the stranded-cost correction)
| Option | DCF operating margin | One-off | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (agreed; still recommended)** | **17.7%** flat | plus the **€500m** one-off restructuring (2027-29) in the bridge | 18.8% less 1.1% ongoing restructuring (FY23-25 actual average), because the 0.6% guide has never been achieved |
| A2 | 18.2% flat | plus €500m | 18.8% less management's 0.6% guide |
| B | 18.8% flat | none | Treats all restructuring as one-off |

Bear case: **16.2%** (the c.17.3% 2023 level less 1.1%), which also covers stranded costs not being fully removed.

Why flat and not rising: the FY26 guide is only a "modest improvement", and much of Unilever's margin history is restructuring savings being spent back on brands. The bear agent should test a +50bps path.
