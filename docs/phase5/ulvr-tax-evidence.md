# Unilever tax evidence (for brief assumption F and data trap 8), 13 October 2026

## 1. Tax rate on operating profit
| | FY24 | FY25 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underlying effective tax rate | 25.9% | **25.7%** | FY25 results 6-K (0001654954-26-001155), non-GAAP reconciliation |
| Reported effective tax rate | 28.7% | 29.4% | same; FY25 "includes the impact of the separation of the Ice Cream business" |

The underlying rate already includes irrecoverable withholding tax on dividends sent up from subsidiaries ("Decreases in non-deductible interest and irrecoverable WHT", FY25 6-K), so it is a fair cash-tax proxy for the ongoing business. **Proposal: 25.7%** (FY25 underlying), as KO and Diageo used their underlying rates.

## 2. Tax and separation costs on the Foods sale: MISSING
- Management: "Unilever's share of the capitalised value of synergies in the combined company fully offsets Unilever's tax and separation costs" (Form 425 presentation, 0000217410-26-000027).
- CFO, 31 March call: "some of the tax costs are subject to the work which we will need to complete over multiple jurisdictions and is subject to valuations. So therefore, at this stage, it will be premature to start making estimates" (Form 425, 0000217410-26-000032).
- The US part is "intended to be tax free" (Reverse Morris Trust); other countries are not covered by that.
- No figure in the FY25 20-F or the H1 2026 report.

**Why management's "offset" argument does not apply to our model:** we value the McCormick shares at today's market price, which already includes whatever synergies investors expect. Counting the synergies and then ignoring the tax would credit the synergies twice. So the tax is a real deduction for us; we just do not know its size.

**Known separately:** the €500m one-off restructuring for stranded costs (2027-29), which goes into the bridge (margin evidence pack).

## 3. Proposal
Because the amount cannot be sourced, the base case **does not deduct it but says so in the memo**, with the sensitivity: **every €1bn of tax and separation cost = 39p a share** (€1bn / 2,154.3m shares x £0.845 per €). The bear case adds a stress of 25% of the $15.7bn cash (UK main corporation tax rate since April 2023, gov.uk), €3.5bn or 137p. That stress is a labelled test, not an estimate: the real figure could be lower (part of the gain may be exempt, and the cash is not all gain) or higher.
