# RELX: terminal growth evidence (14 Oct 2026)

Terminal growth is the rate cash flows grow forever after FY30. It cannot exceed long-run nominal growth of the economies RELX sells into (about 58% North America, the rest Europe and elsewhere: relx-facts.md, FY25R p.26).

| Evidence | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Bank of England inflation target | 2.0% | bankofengland.co.uk, monetary policy remit |
| US Federal Reserve inflation target | 2.0% | federalreserve.gov, statement on longer-run goals |
| US 10-year breakeven inflation (market's inflation expectation) | 2.35% (14 Oct 2026) | FRED T10YIE |
| Desk's portfolio default (Diageo, Coca-Cola) | 2.0% | briefs/DGE.yaml, briefs/KO.yaml |
| Unilever | 1.6% (matched its own lower forecast growth) | briefs/ULVR.yaml, decision 6 |
| RELX forecast growth FY27-30 (agreed) | 5.0% | relx-growth-evidence.md |

No reachable source gives a long-run real growth forecast for RELX's markets (research sites blocked), so any real growth above inflation would be a judgement.

| Option | Terminal growth | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | **2.0%** | Portfolio default; equals the 2% inflation targets, so zero real growth forever. Cautious for a business growing 5%, but the only figure with a direct source |
| B | 2.35% | Market's 10-year US inflation expectation; still zero real growth |
| C | 3.0% | Adds about 1 point of real growth; no direct source |
| Bear | 1.5% | Half a point below inflation: AI erodes pricing power over time |

Note: growth steps down from 5.0% in FY30 to the terminal rate in FY31, as in the Diageo and Coca-Cola models. How much RELX must reinvest to grow forever is set by the terminal return on new investment (a later card).
