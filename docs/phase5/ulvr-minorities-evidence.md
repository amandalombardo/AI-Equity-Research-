**Question:** how much of Unilever's value belongs to outside shareholders in its subsidiaries, mainly Hindustan Unilever (HUL)?

HUL trades at about 44 times its 2025 profit (€41bn / €0.94bn), against roughly 18 times for Unilever as a whole (€120bn market value / €6.8bn underlying profit, FY25). The market pays a large premium for Indian growth.

## The three methods
| Method | Minorities deducted | Per Unilever share | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book value | €2.1bn | c.81p | Fails the evidence test, as it did for Diageo: book value is a fraction of what the 38% is worth on any measure |
| **Proportionate (your Diageo rule)** | Minorities' share of profit x the DCF value | c.10% of the DCF value (8.5-10.1% at Group level; higher on the business that remains, because HUL stays and Foods goes) | Consistent: the DCF values HUL's cash flows at Unilever's growth and cost of capital, and the minorities own their share of exactly those cash flows. The Fundamentals agent computes the exact ratio for the remaining company |
| Market value | 38% of HUL at market, c.€15.7bn, plus others at book | c.616p | An upper bound: it gives the minorities India's c.44x multiple while the DCF values the same business at Unilever's lower multiple. This is the same reason you chose proportionate over market for Diageo's Indian subsidiary |

## Proposal
**Proportionate**, the same rule as Diageo (brief bridge.minorities_method: proportionate). Show HUL-at-market as a bear-case line in the bridge, so the memo states what the stricter view costs.
