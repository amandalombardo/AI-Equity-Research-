# Decision 1: terminal capex for Diageo (Phase 3, 12 Oct 2026)

**The question:** after FY31 the model holds capex at 7.1% of sales forever, about 2.1 times D&A (3.4%). What should capex be in the steady state?

All values are per ordinary share in pence (GBp), and they include the terminal working-capital fix (mistakes log row 9). Everything else is unchanged from the 12 Oct run: WACC 8.46%, growth 2%, price 1,673p.

## Options
| Option | Terminal capex, % of sales | Capex / D&A | Return on new investment it implies | Value | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Capex = D&A (common shortcut) | 3.4% | 1.0x | 65% (not credible) | 1,753p | HOLD (+4.8%) |
| Management's FY27-29 plan ($3.75bn) | 6.6% | 2.0x | 12.1% | 1,510p | HOLD (-9.8%) |
| **New investment earns Diageo's current ROIC (13.4%), RECOMMENDED** | **6.2%** | **1.85x** | **13.4%** | **1,540p** | **HOLD (-8.0%)** |
| Keep the 5-year average (today's model) | 7.1% | 2.1x | 10.8% | 1,474p | SELL (-11.9%) |
| New investment earns only the WACC (bear) | 8.3% | 2.5x | 8.5% | 1,383p | SELL (-17.4%) |

## How to read "return on new investment"
This is the textbook value driver test (McKinsey, *Valuation*): in a steady state, the cash a company must reinvest = growth / return on new investment x NOPAT. If capex is set too low, the model is quietly assuming growth comes almost for free. Capex = D&A implies every new dollar earns 65% a year, which no analyst could defend. Holding 7.1% forever implies new investment earns 10.8%, below what Diageo earns today.

## Evidence
1. **Diageo's history (20-F cash flow statements, SEC EDGAR):** capex / sales FY22-26 = 7.1%, 6.9%, 7.5%, 8.0%, 6.1%. D&A / sales = 3.2%, 2.9%, 3.3%, 3.7%, 3.8%. FY24-25 was a peak (capacity expansion, incl. Guinness). FY26 fell to 6.1% ("more disciplined investment in capex", 20-F FY26 CFO review).
2. **Management's plan:** "$3.75 billion of capital expenditure" over 3 years (20-F FY26, Chair/CEO statement) = 6.6% of our ex-EABL sales. It includes about $670m of Guinness brewing capacity in FY26-29 (trade press on the Capital Markets Day, e.g. drinks-intel.com), which is one-off growth spending, so steady-state capex should be lower than 6.6%.
3. **Diageo's return on invested capital:** 13.4% FY26, 13.7% FY25 (20-F FY26, ROIC KPI). The recommended option assumes new investment earns what the existing business earns.
4. **Industry:** Damodaran's January 2026 US data puts alcoholic beverages at capex of about 2.2x D&A and net capex of about 3.7% of sales (seen through a search summary; the page itself is blocked here, so treat as medium confidence). Diageo's 7.1% - 3.4% = 3.7% is the same, but US peers are also in a capacity build-out (Brown-Forman, Constellation).
5. **Cross-check vs guidance:** the model's FY27-29 cash flow after interest (about $9.2bn) is close to management's guided c.$8bn plus c.$850m of exceptional restructuring cash (= $8.85bn), so near-term capex in the forecast is not out of line. This decision is only about the years after FY31.

## Recommendation
Set terminal capex so new investment earns Diageo's current ROIC (13.4%), which gives 6.2% of sales. It is anchored to a published number and a textbook rule, it sits between FY26 actual capex (6.1%) and the plan that includes one-off Guinness spending (6.6%), and it avoids the "capex = D&A" shortcut that would make growth free. It moves the rating from SELL to HOLD, so it matters: the bear version (returns fall to the WACC) is a SELL at 1,383p and stays in the memo as a risk.
