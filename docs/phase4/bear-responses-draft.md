# Bear case: draft PM responses for the memo (Phase 4)

> **ADOPTED 13 Oct 2026** (Amanda: "correct all the things that need to be corrected"). The final wording, with values updated to the mid-year base of 1,627p, is in the memo. The values below are the end-of-year ones they were drafted on.

The memo currently says "PM to respond" on all six Bear challenges. Below is a draft response for each. Every claim is source-checked in [evidence-phase4.md](evidence-phase4.md), part B. **These are your words in the memo, so change anything you disagree with.** Values are pence per ordinary share (GBp); base case 1,544.8p HOLD, SELL below 1,505.7p.

For each one I also say whether I think the challenge **should change the base case** (my view, for you to accept or reject).

## 1. Flow-through: history says most savings get reinvested (46% vs 33% in 2017)
**Draft response:** "Accepted as the main risk. 46% is the lowest flow-through consistent with management's FY27-29 profit guidance, the same low-end rule we use for sales. Diageo's 2017 rule (two thirds reinvested, about 33% kept) is the SELL line (33.6%), so the HOLD assumes Diageo does slightly better than its own last programme. We will check FY27 interim margins (Feb 2027) against the 46% path."
**Change the base case?** No. The guidance is newer and more specific evidence than the 2017 rule, but the memo should name 33.6% as the trigger.

## 2. Terminal returns: ROIC fell from 18.4% to 13.4% in three years
**Draft response:** "13.4% is Diageo's lowest ROIC in four years, not a recovery assumption. If returns keep falling to 10.8% (what the 5-year capex average implies), value falls 63p to SELL. We hold 13.4% because FY26 capex fell to 6.1% of sales, close to the 6.2% the rule implies."
**Change the base case?** No. 13.4% is the latest observed figure (20-F FY26), and the evidence pack showed it is below every earlier year.

## 3. No recovery: management missed its 2023-26 targets
**Draft response:** "The growth path is already the low end of guidance (0% in FY27), not the midpoint. Zero growth for five years (1,414p, SELL) would mean North America never recovers from -8.4%; we treat that as the bear scenario, not the base."
**Change the base case?** No, but the memo should say plainly that management missed both of its 2023-26 incentive targets, organic sales and organic operating profit growth (20-F p.110), because it cuts the credibility of the low-end rule.

## 4. Terminal growth 2% vs falling spirits volumes
**Draft response:** "2% is nominal growth in US dollars. With US 10-year breakeven inflation at 2.35% (FRED, 8 Oct 2026), it already assumes Diageo shrinks by about 0.35% a year in real terms. Falling volumes need price and mix to keep up with inflation; 1.5% (SELL, -53p) is plausible. The IWSR volume figures are secondhand, so we have not changed the base."
**Change the base case?** No. 2% nominal is already conservative next to inflation; I'd keep it.

## 5. Cost of capital: leverage 3.14x, beta and credit spread
**Draft response:** "Beta 0.96 is the industry bottom-up figure and is above all six regressions of Diageo's own shares (0.37 to 0.77), so it already leans cautious. Diageo says 2.5-3.0x leverage is consistent with an A-band rating (20-F p.186). Our 1% spread is already about the average BBB spread (1.02%, against 0.69% for A-rated bonds; ICE BofA indices via FRED, 8 Oct 2026), and 2% would sit between BBB and high yield (3.15%). Once the USD 3.3bn of disposal proceeds arrive, leverage falls to about 2.6x before tax on the proceeds and before EABL's profit leaves, so somewhat higher in practice (our arithmetic from 20-F figures)."
**Change the base case?** No. The beta flip is close (0.997), but Diageo's own regressions all point lower; only one window's 95% range (0.51 to 1.02, before 2022) reaches 0.96. The 1% spread is already cautious against market spreads.

## 6. USL minority at market price
**Draft response:** "Accepted as a limitation, not a correction. Valuing USL's outside shareholders at market (1,395p) while valuing Diageo's own 57% of USL at the DCF multiple is inconsistent; a fair sum-of-the-parts would raise both sides. The proportionate method values all minorities the same way as the business, and it removed the EABL double count."
**Change the base case?** No. The Bear agent itself calls -150p an upper bound.

## What the Bear case gets right overall
Nine of eleven single changes flip HOLD to SELL; only two (beta 0.72, tax relief) move it up, and nothing reaches BUY. The risk is lopsided to the downside. That matters for your rating call (see the reply in the thread).
